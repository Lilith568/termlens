Osmosis is the passive movement of water molecules across a selectively permeable membrane, from a region of lower solute concentration to one of higher solute concentration. Because the phospholipid bilayer of the cell membrane is largely impermeable to ions, cells rely on transport proteins and aquaporins to regulate their internal environment. When a red blood cell is placed in a hypotonic solution, water diffuses inward and the cell may swell and eventually burst; in a hypertonic solution, it loses water and shrinks. Plant cells, by contrast, are protected by a rigid cell wall, which generates turgor pressure and maintains their structural integrity.
